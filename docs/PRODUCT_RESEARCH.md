# Product research and original design

Official product pages were consulted during implementation. General lessons informed the design; no proprietary code, branding, screenshots or distinctive assets were copied.

- [YNAB method](https://www.ynab.com/the-four-rules): deliberate planning for current needs and irregular future expenses. PennyPilot separates planned allocations from recorded spending.
- [Monarch Money](https://www.monarchmoney.com/): considered as an all-in-one finance reference. Its requested features page did not return readable content in this research session, so no detailed feature claims are attributed to it.
- [Copilot Money](https://copilot.money/): its official page describes transaction categorization, rollovers, cash flow and upcoming bills. PennyPilot uses transparent category totals and explicit projected-versus-paid states, without claiming AI categorization.
- [Actual Budget](https://actualbudget.org/): its official page describes envelope budgeting, data ownership, transfers and reports. PennyPilot adopts exportability and transfer clarity but intentionally uses server-persistent PostgreSQL, not Actual's local-first architecture.

PennyPilot's visual system is original: warm off-white surfaces, restrained green accents, clear cards, accessible form labels and keyboard-operable Radix dialogs. No synthetic financial history is shown in production.
